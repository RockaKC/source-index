## 1. Nothing Stands on One Voice

*Deuteronomy 19:15–19* · thread: The Law of Witness · builds on: none · status: **draft**

**On the front page:** Before Scripture asks anyone to believe anything, it sets the rule for proving it: one voice is never enough. Everything in this series rests on that rule.

### The passage (Septuagint)

> **15** One witness shall not stand to testify against a man for any iniquity, or for any fault, or for any sin which he may commit; by the mouth of two witnesses, or by the mouth of three witnesses, shall every word be established.
> **16** And if an unjust witness rise up against a man, alleging iniquity against him;
> **17** then shall the two men between whom the controversy is, stand before the Lord, and before the priests, and before the judges, who may be in those days.
> **18** And the judges shall make diligent inquiry, and, behold, [if] an unjust witness has borne unjust testimony; [and] has stood up against his brother;
> **19** then shall ye do to him as he wickedly devised to do against his brother, and thou shalt remove the evil from yourselves.

### Context

Deuteronomy is Moses' farewell address to Israel, restating the covenant for a people about to enter the land. Chapter 19 sits in its legal core: cities of refuge for the man who kills without intent, a ban on moving a neighbor's boundary stone, and then the rule on witnesses. All three protect one thing, a community's ability to judge fairly when a life or a livelihood depends on what is said.

The text here is the Septuagint, the Greek Old Testament read by Greek-speaking Jews and by the first Christians. Its wording matters for this series because the New Testament repeats the rule in terms that follow it closely.

### Investigation

The rule is a limit on every single voice. “One witness shall not stand to testify against a man for any iniquity, or for any fault, or for any sin which he may commit”, and the sentence closes with the standard: “by the mouth of two witnesses, or by the mouth of three witnesses, shall every word be established”. Notice the word. Not every verdict: every word. A claim does not become true by being spoken with confidence. It becomes established when a second voice, not the first, says the same thing.

The law repeats itself because it was built to hold under pressure. It governs executions: “He shall die on the testimony of two or three witnesses”, and “one witness shall not testify against a soul that he should die”. Then Deuteronomy 19 turns the rule around and guards the other side. If a witness brings a false charge, “the judges shall make diligent inquiry”, and a proved liar receives what he schemed for his brother: “then shall ye do to him as he wickedly devised to do against his brother”. False witness is not a private failing. It is a crime against the whole community, and it is punished with its own intended sentence.

Now watch what the New Testament does with the rule. Jesus gives it to the church as procedure: “every word may be stood upon the testimony of two or three witnesses”. Paul invokes it of himself: “On the mouth of two or three witnesses every word will be established”. He gives it to Timothy for handling charges against leaders: “Do not receive an accusation against an elder, except upon two or three witnesses”. And Hebrews treats it as settled law: “on the basis of two or three witnesses”. None of them defends the rule. They assume it.

That is the rule behind this series. A witness here is not a quotation to admire. It is a voice that must be confirmed by another. Over the coming days we put the rule to work on Jesus, on an apostle's hearers, on a book most Bibles omit, and last of all on the apostles and angels themselves.

### Cross-references

- **Deuteronomy 17:6**: The same rule at the gravest stakes: no one is put to death on a single voice.  
  “a man who is put to death shall not be put to death for one witness”
- **Numbers 35:30**: The rule appears in the law of murder too, so it is not a quirk of one chapter.  
  “one witness shall not testify against a soul that he should die”
- **Matthew 18:16**: Jesus turns the rule into the church's procedure for a wrong between believers.  
  “every word may be stood upon the testimony of two or three witnesses”
- **2 Corinthians 13:1**: Paul places his own coming visit under the rule.  
  “On the mouth of two or three witnesses every word will be established”
- **Hebrews 10:28**: A New Testament writer treats the rule as established law and builds an argument on it.  
  “dies without mercies on the basis of two or three witnesses”

### Take with you

In Scripture no claim is established by one voice, however sure of itself. It is established when another voice confirms it.

## 2. Even Christ Kept the Rule

*John 8:12–18* · thread: The Law of Witness · builds on: 1 · status: **draft**

**On the front page:** Accused of vouching for himself, Jesus does not claim an exemption. He quotes the law of witness back to his accusers and names his second witness.

### The passage (Berean Literal Bible)

> **12** Again, therefore, Jesus spoke to them, saying, “I am the light of the world. The one following Me shall not walk in the darkness, but will have the light of life.”
> **13** Therefore the Pharisees said to Him, “You testify about Yourself; Your testimony is not true.”
> **14** Jesus answered and said to them, “Even if I testify about Myself, My testimony is true, because I know from where I came and where I am going. But you⁺ do not know from where I come or where I am going.
> **15** You⁺ judge according to the flesh; I judge no one.
> **16** But even if I judge, My judgment is true, because I am not alone—but I and the Father having sent Me.
> **17** And even in your⁺ Law it has been written that the testimony of two men is true.
> **18** I am the One testifying about Myself, and the Father having sent Me testifies about Me.”

### Context

John 8 is one long argument in the temple courts. The closing verses fix the scene: “He spoke these words in the treasury, teaching in the temple”. John tells his Gospel in the language of a courtroom. The words testify and testimony run through chapters 5 and 8, and Jesus' opponents keep demanding proof.

This study follows Deuteronomy 19. There the rule is read in a courtroom. Here it is quoted back by the man on trial.

### Investigation

The objection is exact. “You testify about Yourself; Your testimony is not true”, the Pharisees say. They are not inventing a rule. They are applying Deuteronomy 19:15: one voice cannot establish itself. If Jesus had waved the rule away, he would have conceded that he stood outside the law. He does the opposite.

He answers in two moves. First he defends the quality of his testimony: “Even if I testify about Myself, My testimony is true, because I know from where I came and where I am going”. A witness is only as good as his knowledge, and he claims knowledge his judges lack. Then he meets the rule head on: “And even in your Law it has been written that the testimony of two men is true”. Your law, he says, and he does not correct it. He treats the law of witness as binding on himself.

Then comes the second witness: “I am the One testifying about Myself, and the Father having sent Me testifies about Me”. Two voices, the Son and the Father, satisfy the rule as Moses wrote it. A claim that looked like a man vouching for himself is a claim with two witnesses behind it, one of whom the room cannot see. Their next question shows they grasped the point: “Where is Your Father?”

Carry one puzzle forward. In John 5:31 Jesus says the opposite: “If I testify concerning Myself, My testimony is not true”. The two sentences do not contradict. In chapter 5 he grants the rule's terms and goes on to call witnesses; study 5 follows him through it. In chapter 8 he shows why his testimony stands once the rule is met. Both times he argues under Deuteronomy 19.

### Cross-references

- **Deuteronomy 19:15**: The rule both sides accept. Study 1.  
  “by the mouth of two witnesses, or by the mouth of three witnesses, shall every word be established”
- **John 5:31**: The same concession in the same Gospel. Study 5 follows it.  
  “If I testify concerning Myself, My testimony is not true”
- **John 5:37**: The Father named again as the second witness.  
  “the Father Himself, has testified concerning Me”
- **1 John 5:9**: The apostle John reasons the same way: if we accept human testimony, God's is greater.  
  “the testimony of God is greater”

### Take with you

Jesus does not escape the law of witness. He fulfils it by naming his second witness. If he submitted to the rule, no one else stands outside it.

## 3. Noble Enough to Check the Apostle

*Acts 17:10–12* · thread: The Law of Witness · builds on: 1, 2 · status: **draft**

**On the front page:** Luke calls the Bereans more noble than the Thessalonians, and the reason is that they checked Paul's message against the Scriptures every day.

### The passage (Berean Literal Bible)

> **10** And the brothers sent away both Paul and Silas immediately by night to Berea, who, having arrived, went into the synagogue of the Jews.
> **11** Now these, who were more noble than those in Thessalonica, received the word with all readiness, every day examining the Scriptures, if these things were so.
> **12** Therefore many of them indeed believed, and not a few of the prominent Grecian women and men.

### Context

Acts 17 follows Paul and Silas across Macedonia. In Thessalonica Paul spent “three Sabbaths” reasoning with the synagogue “from the Scriptures”, and trouble sent them away by night. Berea is the next stop.

When Luke writes 'the Scriptures' here, he means the Law, the Prophets and the Writings: the books Paul could open in a synagogue. The New Testament did not yet exist. The only standard on the table was the Old Testament, and Paul argued from it, “opening and setting forth that it was necessary for the Christ to have suffered and to have risen out from the dead”.

### Investigation

Luke pays the Bereans a compliment no church leader hands out lightly: “more noble than those in Thessalonica”. The reason follows at once, in two parts. They “received the word with all readiness”, so they were not hostile. And they were “every day examining the Scriptures, if these things were so”, so they were not gullible. Readiness and examination, together. Take either away and the nobility goes with it.

Look at what the examining was aimed at. Not a doubt about Scripture, but a question put to Paul: were these things so? The Bereans treated an apostle's message as a claim that Scripture could confirm or fail to confirm. That is Deuteronomy 19:15 in the hands of ordinary listeners. Scripture was the second witness, and they went looking for it daily. Paul had made that possible: his own method was to reason from the text, not to demand trust in his office.

Now read the result: “many of them indeed believed”. The checking did not drive them away from faith. It led them into it, on ground they had verified for themselves.

That habit is the one this series asks of you: a passage a day, the text open, and the question put to each claim, ours included. Is it so?

### Cross-references

- **Acts 17:2–3**: Paul's own method in the city before Berea: reasoning from the Scriptures, so his message could be checked there.  
  “he reasoned with them from the Scriptures”
- **1 Thessalonians 5:21**: Paul's letter to the church he had just left states the Bereans' habit as a command.  
  “test all things. Hold fast to the good”
- **1 John 4:1**: John gives the same discipline as a rule for spirits and prophets.  
  “do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits”
- **Luke 24:27**: Christ had already taught that the Scriptures speak of him, which is what Paul was reasoning from.  
  “He interpreted to them the things concerning Himself in all the Scriptures”

### Take with you

The New Testament praises listeners who checked an apostle against the Scriptures. Readiness and examination belong together.

## 4. The Witness Jude Called

*Jude 1:14–15* · thread: The Law of Witness · builds on: 1 · status: **draft**

**On the front page:** To make his case, Jude calls a witness from a book most Bibles leave out. The words he introduces as prophecy appear in 1 Enoch, which the Ethiopian Church kept.

### The passage (Berean Literal Bible)

> **14** And Enoch, the seventh from Adam, also prophesied as to these, saying: “Behold, the Lord came amidst myriads of His holy ones,
> **15** to do judgment against all, and to convict all the ungodly concerning all their works of ungodliness that they did in an ungodly way, and concerning all the harsh [things] that ungodly sinners spoke against Him.”

### Context

Jude is a one-chapter letter from the brother of James, written in haste. His reason is plain: “contend earnestly for the faith having been delivered once for all to the saints”, because “certain men came in stealthily”. Verses 5 to 19 are his evidence: a people saved from Egypt and later destroyed, angels who left their place, Sodom, Cain, Balaam, Korah. Enoch is the last witness he calls.

Enoch is named in , where he walks with God and is taken. The book of 1 Enoch is attributed to him and survives complete in Ge'ez, in the Ethiopian Orthodox Church. The English text of 1 Enoch in this archive is R. H. Charles' translation.

### Investigation

Jude writes “And Enoch, the seventh from Adam, also prophesied as to these, saying”. He does not write 'as some say' or 'as the story goes'. He says Enoch prophesied, and then he quotes. Jude is calling a witness, as the law of witness requires, to stand beside the Scriptures his readers already held.

Now lay the two texts side by side. Jude: “Behold, the Lord came amidst myriads of His holy ones”, “to do judgment against all, and to convict all the ungodly”. First Enoch 1:9: “He cometh with ten thousands of His holy ones”, “To execute judgement upon all, And to destroy all the ungodly: And to convict all flesh”. The coming with holy ones, the judgment on all, the conviction of the ungodly for their works and for the hard things sinners have spoken against Him: all of it is in both. One is Greek rendered into English and the other is Ge'ez rendered into English, so we should not expect identical words. We should expect the same content, and we find it.

What this shows, and what it does not. It shows that an apostle's letter, which every Bible includes, treats words found in 1 Enoch as prophecy and uses them as evidence in an argument about judgment. It does not, by itself, settle whether Jude meant to certify the whole book. That is the canon question, and Kingdom Code's essays take it up at length. The investigation starts with what is in front of us: Jude had Enoch's words, and he called them prophecy.

Enoch says what his testimony was for: “not for this generation, but for a remote one which is for to come”. A witness addressed to a later generation is a witness that has to be kept until it is needed. Study 7 returns to what it takes to keep it.

### Cross-references

- **Enoch 1:9**: The passage Jude's words parallel, in the archive's text of 1 Enoch.  
  “He cometh with ten thousands of His holy ones”
- **Enoch 1:2**: The book states its own audience: a later generation.  
  “not for this generation, but for a remote one which is for to come”
- **Jude 1:3**: Jude's reason for writing: the faith delivered once, now under threat.  
  “contend earnestly for the faith having been delivered once for all to the saints”
- **Genesis 5:24**: Genesis is where Enoch is named. Counting the genealogy from Adam, he is the seventh, as Jude says.  
  “Enoch was well-pleasing to God, and was not found, because God translated him”

### Take with you

Jude introduces words found in 1 Enoch with the word 'prophesied'. Whatever one concludes about the whole book, the New Testament itself puts this testimony on the table.

## 5. A Courtroom With Five Witnesses

*John 5:31–40, 45–47* · thread: The Law of Witness · builds on: 1, 2 · status: **draft**

**On the front page:** Jesus opens by granting that one voice cannot stand, then calls his witnesses in order. The fifth is the Old Testament itself.

### The passage (Berean Literal Bible)

> **31** If I testify concerning Myself, My testimony is not true.
> **32** It is another testifying concerning Me, and I know that the testimony that He testifies concerning Me is true.
> **33** You⁺ have sent to John, and he has testified to the truth.
> **34** Now I do not receive testimony from man, but I say these things so that you⁺ may be saved.
> **35** He was the lamp burning and shining, and you⁺ were willing to exult for an hour in his light.
> **36** But I have testimony greater than that of John. For the works that the Father has given to Me so that I should complete them, the same works that I do, testify concerning Me that the Father has sent Me.
> **37** And the One having sent Me, the Father Himself, has testified concerning Me. You⁺ have neither heard His voice at any time, nor have you⁺ seen His form.
> **38** And you⁺ do not have His word remaining in you⁺, for you⁺ do not believe Him whom He sent.
> **39** You⁺ search the Scriptures because you⁺ think to have eternal life in them, and these are they testifying concerning Me;
> **40** and you⁺ are not willing to come to Me so that you⁺ may have life.

> **45** Do not think that I will accuse you⁺ to the Father. There is the one accusing you⁺: Moses, in whom you⁺ have hoped.
> **46** For if you⁺ were believing Moses, you⁺ would have been believing Me; for he wrote concerning Me.
> **47** But if you⁺ do not believe his writings, how will you⁺ believe My words?”

### Context

John 5 begins with a healing on the Sabbath and the anger it provokes. From verse 19 Jesus explains his work and his claims. Verses 31 to 47 are his case: the evidence for what he has said.

This is the rule of Study 2 from the other side. In chapter 8 the second witness was the Father. In chapter 5 he lines up the whole bench.

### Investigation

He starts by granting the rule, in terms that sound like self-defeat: “If I testify concerning Myself, My testimony is not true”. This is the law of witness stated against himself, on purpose. He will not ask for belief on his own word. He will call others.

Count them. First, John the Baptist: “he has testified to the truth”. Second, his works: “the same works that I do, testify concerning Me”. Third, the Father: “the Father Himself, has testified concerning Me”. Fourth, the Scriptures: “these are they testifying concerning Me”. Fifth, Moses: “he wrote concerning Me”. Five voices of five different kinds: a man, a deed, God, a book, and the lawgiver.

The fourth and fifth witnesses are the reason this series reads the Old Testament as testimony. His opponents searched the Scriptures carefully, “because you think to have eternal life in them”, and still “are not willing to come to Me”. They held the witness in their hands and would not hear what it said. Reading the text was not their failure. Reading it as an end, and not as testimony that points beyond itself, was.

Then Moses stops being a distant authority and becomes a witness in court: “There is the one accusing you: Moses, in whom you have hoped”. The verdict: “But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe My words?” Christ's authority is argued from the Old Testament's, not set against it.

### Cross-references

- **John 8:17–18**: Study 2: the two-witness rule in chapter 8, with the Father as the second witness.  
  “the testimony of two men is true”
- **Luke 24:27**: After the resurrection Christ does what he does here: he reads the Old Testament as testimony about himself.  
  “He interpreted to them the things concerning Himself in all the Scriptures”
- **Luke 24:44**: He names the three divisions of the Old Testament that bear witness.  
  “all things having been written concerning Me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms”
- **Deuteronomy 18:15**: One place where Moses writes of a prophet like himself whom the people are told to hear.  
  “The Lord thy God shall raise up to thee a prophet of thy brethren, like me; him shall ye hear”

### Take with you

Christ calls five witnesses and the Old Testament is among them. To hold Moses and the Prophets in your hands and refuse to hear what they say of him is the failure he names here.

## 6. Babylon's Appetite for Witnesses

*Revelation 17:1–6* · thread: The Law of Witness · builds on: 1, 2, 5 · status: **draft**

**On the front page:** The English word martyr comes from the Greek word for witness. Revelation names Babylon's crime against exactly those people.

### The passage (Berean Literal Bible)

> **1** And one of the seven angels having the seven bowls came and spoke with me, saying, “Come here, I will show you the judgment of the great prostitute, the one sitting upon many waters,
> **2** with whom the kings of the earth committed sexual immorality, and those dwelling on the earth were made drunk with the wine of her sexual immorality.”
> **3** And he carried me away in the Spirit into a wilderness, and I saw a woman sitting upon a scarlet beast being full of names of blasphemy, having seven heads and ten horns.
> **4** And the woman was clothed in purple and scarlet, and adorned with gold and precious stone and pearls, holding a golden cup in her hand, being full of abominations and the impurities of her sexual immorality.
> **5** And upon her forehead a name was written, a mystery: BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF THE PROSTITUTES AND OF THE ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH.
> **6** And I saw the woman being drunk with the blood of the saints and with the blood of the witnesses of Jesus. And having seen her, I marveled with great wonder.

### Context

Revelation 17 and 18 give the vision of Babylon the Great and her fall. The angel offers to show “the judgment of the great prostitute”, and the chapter then describes her seat, her dress, her cup and her name. The name is the key: “a mystery: BABYLON THE GREAT”.

The book is addressed to churches under pressure from an empire that demanded loyalty. Throughout it 'witness' is a heavily loaded word, and this passage ends on it.

### Investigation

Read the description as a charge sheet. The people of the earth were “made drunk with the wine of her sexual immorality”. She is rich, “clothed in purple and scarlet, and adorned with gold and precious stone and pearls”. And then the verdict on her record: “drunk with the blood of the saints and with the blood of the witnesses of Jesus”.

The Greek word behind 'witnesses' is martys. It is the courtroom word of the last five studies, and it is the source of the English word martyr. Follow it through the New Testament and the cost appears. In  Jesus tells his followers they will be his witnesses, and Stephen is spoken of as “Your witness Stephen”. In Pergamum there is “Antipas My witness, My faithful one, who was killed”. And Jesus Christ himself is “the faithful witness”.

Read together, the images give a picture of an order that intoxicates the many and kills the witnesses who will not drink. Revelation 18:24 gives the total: “in her was found the blood of prophets and of saints and of all those having been slain on the earth”. This is the Scripture behind Kingdom Code's use of the name Babylon: not a nickname for a city, but the name Revelation gives to a system that runs on consensus and answers a faithful witness with violence.

The Early Church section of this archive begins with two men remembered as martyrs, Ignatius of Antioch and Justin Martyr. The word in Justin's name is the word in this passage.

### Cross-references

- **Acts 1:8**: Where the word starts for the church: a commission to testify, before there is any cost.  
  “you will be My witnesses”
- **Acts 22:20**: The first named witness in Acts whose blood is poured out.  
  “the blood of Your witness Stephen was poured out”
- **Revelation 1:5**: Christ is the pattern: the faithful witness before his witnesses.  
  “Jesus Christ, the faithful witness”
- **Revelation 2:13**: A named witness killed in a church's city, so the chapter is not only about the future.  
  “Antipas My witness, My faithful one, who was killed”
- **Revelation 18:24**: The accounting of Babylon's guilt that closes her fall.  
  “in her was found the blood of prophets and of saints”

### Take with you

Scripture's word for a witness is the word that became martyr, and Revelation lays the blood of the witnesses at Babylon's door.

## 7. Not Even an Angel

*Galatians 1:6–9* · thread: The Law of Witness · builds on: 1, 3, 4 · status: **draft**

**On the front page:** Paul puts himself and an angel from heaven under the same test: a message is measured against what was delivered, not against who delivers it.

### The passage (Berean Literal Bible)

> **6** I marvel that so quickly you⁺ are deserting from the One having called you⁺ in the grace of Christ to another gospel,
> **7** which is not another, except there are some who are troubling you⁺ and are wanting to pervert the gospel of Christ.
> **8** But even if we or an angel out of heaven should preach a gospel to you⁺ contrary to what we preached to you⁺, let him be accursed!
> **9** As we have said before, even now I say again: If anyone preaches a gospel [to] you⁺ contrary to what you⁺ received, let him be accursed!

### Context

Galatians is Paul's most urgent letter. He skips his usual thanksgiving and goes straight to the charge: “I marvel that so quickly you are deserting from the One having called you in the grace of Christ to another gospel”. Churches he had planted were being turned toward a different message by teachers who came after him.

Chapters 1 and 2 are Paul's defense, and they are built on the law of witness. He tells how he laid his message before the leaders in Jerusalem, “lest somehow I might be running or had run in vain”, and how James, Cephas and John gave him “the right hands of fellowship”. His gospel was confirmed by other voices, as the rule requires.

### Investigation

Read verse 8 slowly: “even if we or an angel out of heaven should preach a gospel to you contrary to what we preached to you, let him be accursed”. Paul names three possible sources of a false gospel: another teacher, himself, and a messenger from heaven. The highest authority conceivable goes into the test. If Paul himself changed the message, the Galatians were to reject him.

The measure is not the speaker. It is “what you received”. This is Jude's “faith having been delivered once for all to the saints” seen from the receiving end: a message that was delivered, a people who received it, and a standard that does not move when a new voice arrives. Rank cannot be a witness. An apostle, an angel, a scholar, a council, a bestselling author: each is one voice, and one voice is not enough.

Put the week together. Deuteronomy gave the rule: two or three witnesses. Jesus submitted to it. The Bereans applied it to an apostle. Jude called a witness from a book the Ethiopian canon kept. Jesus listed five witnesses and counted Moses among them. Revelation shows what the world does to faithful witnesses. And Paul turns the rule on himself. Scripture is not a stack of statements to be believed on authority. It is a body of testimony that confirms itself, and it asks the reader to take part in the checking.

One warning from the Ethiopian canon names what the test protects against: “sinners will alter and pervert the words of righteousness in many ways”. Alteration is the threat. That is why the words of the testimony matter, and why this archive keeps them open to read.

### Cross-references

- **2 Corinthians 13:1**: Paul puts his own coming visit under the rule of two or three witnesses.  
  “On the mouth of two or three witnesses every word will be established”
- **Acts 17:11**: Study 3: ordinary listeners applying the same test to an apostle.  
  “every day examining the Scriptures, if these things were so”
- **Jude 1:3**: Study 4: the faith delivered once, the standard Paul appeals to.  
  “the faith having been delivered once for all to the saints”
- **1 John 4:1**: The same discipline given to the whole church as a command.  
  “do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits”
- **Enoch 104:10**: Alteration of the words is the danger that makes the test necessary.  
  “sinners will alter and pervert the words of righteousness in many ways”

### Take with you

Rank cannot be a witness. Every voice, apostle and angel included, is measured against what was delivered and confirmed by others.
